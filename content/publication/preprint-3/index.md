abstract: >
  We introduce a notion of intrinsic differentiability for functionals on the space of finite nonnegative measures equipped with the Wasserstein–Fisher–Rao (WFR) distance, also known as the Hellinger–Kantorovich (HK) distance. The resulting static first-order calculus accounts simultaneously for spatial transport and mass reweighting. We characterize regular barycentric logarithms through optimal semicouplings and measurable selections at the transport threshold, and prove their tangency at arbitrary finite source measures. The defining expansion extends to non-optimal semicouplings, with remainder of smaller order than the square root of their cost. This yields differentiation formulas for simultaneous transport and reweighting and uniqueness of the gradients. We also give a sufficient criterion in terms of regular flat derivatives. We characterize differentiability of one half the squared WFR distance by a common optimal Monge transport–growth representation with no target mass created from the cone vertex. Equivalently, the regular barycentric logarithm is unique and its norm equals the distance; the gradient is minus this logarithm. Finally, we formulate the corresponding calculus on probability measures. On $\mathcal{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^d)$, WFR differentiability implies intrinsic Wasserstein differentiability, with the transport component of the WFR gradient coinciding with the Wasserstein gradient. Whenever a flat derivative exists, one quarter of the reaction component agrees almost everywhere with its centered representative. As a byproduct we obtain a Wasserstein differentiability result for the squared WFR distance.
